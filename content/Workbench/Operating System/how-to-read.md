---
title: How to Read
---


**Purpose**  
Reading is not for accumulation. It is for building a system of connected, usable ideas.

---

**Core Orientation**  
I read to trigger **pattern recognition**:

- **Recognition** — something I’ve felt or seen becomes clearer
    
- **Novelty** — something new connects to what I already know
    

If neither happens, I move on.

---

**Principles**

- **Connection over collection**  
    If it doesn’t connect, it doesn’t compound.
    
- **Patterns over recall**  
    I don’t retain books—I retain patterns that emerge across them.
    
- **Refinement over capture**  
    Raw ideas are inputs. Only processed insights belong in the refinery.
    

---

**Heuristics**

- While reading:  
    → _What does this connect to?_
    
    
- After reading:  
    → _What clicked?_
    
- For each spark:  
    → _Why did this spark?_  
    (clarified something, connected ideas, or challenged an assumption)
    

---

**Constraint**

From any source, extract **one** non-obvious connection.  
That is enough.

---

**Refinery Output Standard**

An idea is only kept if it becomes one of:

- a **principle** (guides decisions)
    
- a **heuristic** (fast rule of thumb)
    
- a **model** (explains how something works)
    
- a **constraint** (what I avoid)
    
- a **feedback loop** (how I evaluate)
    

---

**Operating Rule**

Before trying to remember more, I focus on making **one thing clearer**.

---

**Reminder**

I am not building a library.  
I am building an operating system.
